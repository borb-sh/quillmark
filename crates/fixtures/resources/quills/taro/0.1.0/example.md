Taro ice cream starts with a corm, not a bean. The corm steams until it gives
under a spoon, then goes through a sieve fine enough that no fibre survives
into the custard. What comes out is pale lavender and tastes faintly of
chestnut.

The colour is the part people argue about. A custard built from corm alone
settles at a grey-violet that photographs badly, so most shops correct it. The
purists do not, and theirs are the scoops worth queueing for.

## Serving

- One scoop, no cone. The cone's malt fights the chestnut note.
- Salted cream on top, if anything at all.
