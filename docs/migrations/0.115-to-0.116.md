# 0.115 → 0.116 — a card no kind claims, and a body under `body.enabled: false`, render and warn; an undeclared key warns; a non-mapping card-yaml payload fails at its fence

The break nothing reports: **a document that failed to render now renders.**
A card with no `$kind`, a card whose `$kind` the quill does not declare, and
body prose under `body.enabled: false` were fatal `validation::*` errors. Each
is now a `Warning` from `quill.validate(doc)`, and the render proceeds without
the input. A pipeline that relied on the render failing to catch these
documents catches nothing: run `quill.validate(doc)` and act on the three
codes below. On the CLI, `render` prints them, and `quillmark check --strict`
fails on them.

The rule behind it: a render fails only where the engine would have to invent
what the author wrote. Input no declaration claims displaces nothing, so it
renders and warns
([SCHEMAS.md § What blocks a render](https://github.com/borb-sh/quillmark/blob/main/prose/canon/SCHEMAS.md#what-blocks-a-render)).

| Break | Surface | Action |
|---|---|---|

| Body prose under `body.enabled: false` renders without the body instead of failing | rendered output | Same, for `validation::body_disabled` |
| A card with no `$kind` is `validation::kindless_card`, not `validation::unknown_card` with `card: ""` | diagnostics | Route on the new code. Its `args` carry `allowed`, the quill's declared kinds |

## A date field may hold `today`

A document dated by the day it renders stores `today` in a `date` field, or the
quill declares `default: today`. The field renders as the clock's UTC date, the
date a plate's `datetime.today()` returns.

## What reaches the plate

A card no kind claims keeps its place in `$cards`, fields verbatim and
uncoerced, with its `$kind` if it names one and no `$body`. A plate that reads
the discriminator with `card.at("$kind", default: none)` and branches on the
kinds it knows falls through on it, which is what every fixture plate does. A
plate reading `card.at("$kind")` bare fails to compile on a kindless card:
read it with a default.

A body under `body.enabled: false` does not reach the plate, as before.

## An undeclared key warns

`quill.validate(doc)` warns `validation::unknown_field` at each key the schema
does not declare where it sits: a card field, a typed dictionary's property, an
array element's property, a matrix member's column, or a cell inside a variant
container that no world declares. The render is unchanged: the key still
crosses to the plate verbatim, except inside a variant container, whose wire
carries only the live world.

A strict consumer that reads an empty `validate` as done sees the new warning
on any document carrying such a key. That includes a key a plate reads through
`data.at("logo", default: none)` without declaring it: declare it in
`Quill.yaml`, and the warning goes.

The hint names the likely fix, and `args` carry it. `container` and `variant`
mark a variant cell written beside its discriminant (`outcome: motion` with
`moved_by:` next to it rather than under it). `suggestion` names the declared
field a misspelled key most likely meant.

## A non-mapping payload has its own code

A column-zero `~~~` opens a card-yaml block whatever its info string, so code
fenced with tildes in a body reaches the YAML parser, where it usually reads as
a string. The failure carried no location and named neither rule nor fix. It is
now its own code, located at the opener's line, and its hint names the backtick
fence to write instead.

| A block holding | 0.115 | 0.116 |
|---|---|---|
| a string, number, boolean or sequence, as a card | `parse::invalid_structure`, `Invalid YAML in card block: expected a mapping`, no location | `parse::payload_not_mapping`, located at the opener |
| the same, as the root block | `parse::missing_quill` | `parse::payload_not_mapping`, located at the opener |
| an empty or null payload | an empty mapping | unchanged |

`args` carries `actual` (`string`, `number`, `boolean` or `sequence`) and, when
the opener has an info string, `info`: `python` for `~~~python`.

**Migrate:** a consumer routing the non-mapping case under
`parse::invalid_structure` or `parse::missing_quill` routes it under
`parse::payload_not_mapping`. To fix a document, change each tilde fence
around code to backticks: the diagnostic's line is the one to edit.

## `paint` takes one scale

`paint` rasterized at `layoutScale × densityScale` and reported
`layoutWidth = widthPt × layoutScale` back, a number the caller computed to
pass in. It takes the product alone, backing-store pixels per point, as a
required argument, and returns `undefined`.

```ts
// 0.115
const r = session.paint(ctx, page, { layoutScale, densityScale });
canvas.style.width = `${r.layoutWidth}px`;
canvas.style.height = `${r.layoutHeight}px`;

// 0.116
session.paint(ctx, page, layoutScale * densityScale);
canvas.style.width = `${widthPt * layoutScale}px`; // or "100%" of a page box
canvas.style.height = `${heightPt * layoutScale}px`;
```

A call with no scale, or with an options object, throws
`backend::invalid_raster_scale` where the first painted at 1 and the second at
the options' product. A scale that is not finite and positive throws the same
code, where it threw an uncoded message.

The painter still reduces a scale that would put a side past 16384 px. A
consumer that read `clamped`, `effectiveDensityScale` or `pixelWidth` /
`pixelHeight` reads `canvas.width` / `canvas.height`, and the scale painted at
is `canvas.width / widthPt`.

## One raster ceiling

Core refused a raster past 16384² px of area (`MAX_RASTER_PIXELS`) and the
WASM painter clamped at 16384 px a side, a limit of its own. The side is the
one limit now, core's `MAX_RASTER_SIDE`, and `fit_raster_scale` is the
reduction `paint` applies. The side rule is the narrower of the two only for a
page long on one axis: a PNG export whose long side passes 16384 px at the
asked `ppi` fails under `backend::invalid_raster_scale`, where it rendered
while its area fit.
